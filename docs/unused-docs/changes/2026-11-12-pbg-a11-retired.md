# PBG-A11 retired — the two flat themes given a subtle surface

- Date: 2026-11-12
- Retired by: SEP-M1 - SEP-M3, `docs/unused-docs/implementation_plan_2026-11-12_surface_separation.md`

The decision the entry said was the user's to make was made: separate the
two surfaces, using colours each theme already ships. `apple_grey`'s
`CTkFrame.top_fg_color` became `["#F5F5F7", "#2C2C2E"]` and
`black_white`'s became `["#F3F4F6", "#111111"]`, in
`tools/generate_ctk_themes.py` where the entry correctly said the change
belonged. Measured separation 1.0888 / 1.2076 and 1.1005 / 1.1121, against
the 1.1068 - 1.2364 the eight themes that were never flat already spanned.

Two of the entry's own claims did not survive the measurement, and they are
worth keeping because they are what deferred the fix:

- "`black_white` has no grey to spend by design" — it already spends
  `#D1D5DB`, `#9CA3AF`, `#6B7280`, `#4B5563`, `#F3F4F6` and `#111111` on
  borders, entries and anchors. The subtle surface takes two it already had.
- "it needs a decision rather than a patch" — true as filed, but the
  decision turned out not to need a new colour: `apple_grey` already
  declared a three-level stack (`CTk.fg_color` `#F5F5F7`,
  `CTkFrame.fg_color` `#FFFFFF`) with `top_fg_color` repeating the middle
  step instead of taking the third. `#2C2C2E` is the only new literal in
  the batch.

The guard was inverted rather than deleted:
`test_pbga11_two_bundled_themes_have_one_surface_where_the_app_wants_two`
asserted the flat set was exactly those two, which pinned the bug in place
once the decision was made. It is now
`test_sepm2_no_bundled_theme_has_one_surface_where_the_app_wants_two` and
asserts the empty set, so a theme that flattens from here fails whichever
one it is.

One thing the fix substitutes rather than removes, flagged in the plan's
section 5 and not guarded: on `apple_grey` light the subtle surface is now
`#F5F5F7`, which is also that theme's window fill — so a `surface_subtle`
widget placed straight on the window, rather than inside a `#FFFFFF` frame,
is flat against it. Narrower than what it replaced (every row, every pane)
and it is Apple's own model, but no test looks for it.

## The entry as it was filed

### PBG-A11 — two bundled themes make one surface where the app wants two (medium)

Found by CI on 2026-11-12, after the batch was pushed. `apple_grey` and
`black_white` set `CTkFrame.top_fg_color` equal to `CTkFrame.fg_color` in both
modes — `#FFFFFF`/`#1D1D1F` and `#FFFFFF`/`#000000` — and those are the two
tokens `theme.py` exposes as `surface_subtle` and `surface`. They are the only
thing separating a list row from the pane behind it and a Plan chip from its
box, so on those two themes there is no edge to be had whichever token the
code picks. Counted by
`tests/test_time_block_colors.py::test_pbga11_two_bundled_themes_have_one_surface_where_the_app_wants_two`,
an exact set, which fails if a third theme flattens.

App-wide and older than this batch: every list row in the app is the colour of
its pane on those two themes. The Plan screen only joined them when PBG-M3
took away the priority tint that had been separating its rows.

Not fixed here because the fix is a shipped colour: `apple_grey` would want
Apple's own `#F5F5F7` for the subtle surface, and `black_white` has no grey to
spend by design, so it needs a decision rather than a patch. `themes/*.json`
are generated by `tools/generate_ctk_themes.py`, so the change belongs there
and not in the JSON.

Beside it, how it was found: `test_pbga4` and `test_pbga7` compared the two
resolved colours while pinning only the appearance mode, so they measured
whichever theme the *suite* had left loaded — green on the files a change
touches, red on CI, same commit. Both now take a `two_tone_theme` fixture.
`test_pdm9` was already safe, because it reads all ten themes out of their
JSON rather than the loaded one.

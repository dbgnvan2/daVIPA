## 1. What this batch is

`BACKLOG.md`'s **PBG-A11**, filed by the lunch session after CI caught it, and
answered by the user's "fix it here".

`apple_grey` and `black_white` set `CTkFrame.top_fg_color` equal to
`CTkFrame.fg_color` in both modes. Those are the two tokens `theme.py` exposes
as `surface_subtle` and `surface`, and they are the only thing separating a
list row from the pane behind it and a Plan chip from its box. Measured across
the ten bundled themes:

| | separation ratio |
|---|---|
| `apple_grey` light and dark | **1.0000** |
| `black_white` light and dark | **1.0000** |
| the other eight, both modes | 1.1068 – 1.2364 |

`apple_grey` is `DEFAULT_THEME_NAME` and the user's own theme; their
`appearance_mode` is `system` with macOS in Light. So this is live on their
machine, in both modes, on the default theme — and it is **app-wide**, not a
Plan-screen bug: every list row in the app is the colour of its pane on those
two themes. PBG-M3 only made the Plan screen join them, by removing the
priority tint that had been separating its rows.

### 1.1 Why this revision abandons revision 1's approach

Revision 1 proposed deriving a separated `surface_subtle` inside
`semantic_colors()` whenever a theme made the two tokens equal, with a computed
nudge (`#FFFFFF`→`#F1F1F1`, and so on) at a 1.12 target.

The lunch session's PBG-A11 entry makes a better argument, and I am taking it:
the fix is a **shipped colour**, `themes/*.json` are generated by
`tools/generate_ctk_themes.py`, so the change belongs in the generator and not
in a code path that overrides what a theme asked for. Two things I then
measured settle it:

- **Each theme already contains the right value.** `apple_grey`'s generator
  block already declares a three-level stack — `CTk.fg_color` `#F5F5F7`,
  `CTkFrame.fg_color` `#FFFFFF`, and `top_fg_color` duplicating the frame
  instead of taking the third step. `#F5F5F7` appears 5 times in the theme
  already. `black_white` already ships `#F3F4F6` and `#111111` as its anchor
  pair. So no new palette decision is needed, which was the stated reason
  PBG-A11 was deferred rather than fixed.
- **"`black_white` has no grey to spend by design" is not quite so.** That
  theme already spends `#D1D5DB`, `#9CA3AF`, `#6B7280`, `#4B5563`, `#F3F4F6`
  and `#111111` on borders, entries and anchors. Giving its subtle surface a
  grey it already ships is consistent with the theme rather than a betrayal of
  it. This also disposes of revision 1's "keep `black_white` pure" alternative:
  it is not pure now.

So: designed colours in the generator, no code override. Revision 1's
`semantic_colors()` floor is **dropped** — it was machinery for a case no
bundled theme will hit once the data is right, and `test_pbga11` already
guards the bundled set at the JSON level. Dropping it also keeps a theme free
to mean what it says.

## 2. Tier and budget, stated before the work

No line under `src/` changes: the diff is `tools/generate_ctk_themes.py`, two
generated `themes/*.json`, one test, and documents. By the letter of
`CLAUDE.md`'s *Review sweeps* — "tests, config or a document alone earns no
sweep at all" — that reads as no sweep.

I am taking the **Full** tier anyway, and saying why rather than leaving it
implicit: this changes a shipped surface colour on the default theme, which
`semantic_colors()` feeds to 39 call sites across 15 modules, so what it can
break is every list and panel in the app. The path it touches is config; the
blast radius is not. `effort-budget.md` §1 says to decide the tier from what
the change can break, and that is the reading I am applying.

So: at most 2 warm passes, **at least 1 cold pass**, a further pass only on a
high-severity finding and then cold and in a different failure family.

`effort-budget.md` §9: a fix batch is counted by findings, at most one guard
per finding. One finding (PBG-A11); this plan **inverts one existing guard and
adds none**.

## 4. How each is built

**SEP-M1.** Two lines in `APPLE_NEUTRAL_OVERRIDES` and
`BLACK_WHITE_NEUTRAL_OVERRIDES`:

| theme | `top_fg_color` now | proposed | separation | text on it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `apple_grey` | `["#FFFFFF", "#1D1D1F"]` | `["#F5F5F7", "#2C2C2E"]` | 1.0888 / 1.2076 | 15.46:1 / 12.80:1 |
| `black_white` | `["#FFFFFF", "#000000"]` | `["#F3F4F6", "#111111"]` | 1.1005 / 1.1121 | 19.08:1 / 18.88:1 |

Every value except `#2C2C2E` is already in its theme. `#2C2C2E` is Apple's own
dark elevation step and sits between the theme's `#1D1D1F` surface and its
`#424245` border; `#424245` itself was the alternative and measures 1.6805,
which is a border's worth of contrast rather than a surface's.

Three of the four land in or at the 1.1068–1.2364 band the eight healthy
themes occupy. **`apple_grey` light is the exception at 1.0888**, about 2%
under the band floor. Taken deliberately: white cards on `#F5F5F7` is Apple's
own grouped-background pairing, which is what the theme is imitating, and the
band is a description of eight other themes rather than a specification. If
the sweep or the user's own eye finds it too faint, the fallback is a value
off the theme's ramp rather than a computed one.

**SEP-M2.** `test_pbga11` currently asserts
`flat == {"apple_grey", "black_white"}` — it pins the bug in place, which was
right while the decision was outstanding and is wrong once it is made. It
becomes `assert flat == set()`, with the message naming any theme that
flattened. Same shape, same JSON-level reading, opposite claim. Its docstring
loses "there is no edge to be had whichever token the code picks", which the
fix falsifies.

**SEP-M3.** Run `./venv/bin/python tools/generate_ctk_themes.py`, commit what
it writes. Then run it a second time and confirm `git diff` is empty, which is
the thing that proves the JSON came from the generator rather than from me.

**SEP-M4.** No work — `test_pdm9` already walks all ten themes out of their
JSON and checks mark legibility, so it covers the new values for free. It is in
§7's run list for that reason. The numbers above are the pre-measurement.

**SEP-D1.** PBG-A11 leaves `BACKLOG.md` with the reason, per the repo's rule
that an item that is done leaves with its reason, to `docs/unused-docs/`. The
`two_tone_theme` fixture docstring keeps the fixture (it still makes those two
tests deterministic) but stops saying the flat themes cannot be fixed.

## 5. Criteria not made code-testable, flagged

- **SEP-D1** is prose plus a generated file; nothing reads `BACKLOG.md` or
  `docs/spec_coverage.md` (`effort-budget.md` §5).
- **One consequence no test will catch**, flagged for the sweep rather than
  guarded: on `apple_grey` light, `surface_subtle` becomes `#F5F5F7`, which is
  also `CTk.fg_color` — the window background. Anywhere a `surface_subtle`
  widget sits directly on the window rather than inside a `#FFFFFF` frame, it
  will be flat against it. That is a far narrower surface than the present bug
  (every row, every pane) and it is Apple's own model, but it is a real
  substitution of one flatness for another in some unknown number of places.
  The sweep looks for it; the §7 checklist is where it would show.
- **Still unverified by anything, five batches deep:** the CustomTkinter UI
  contract (`~/.claude/standards/ui-regression.md`). No automated check looks
  at a rendered screen, and this batch changes a colour under 15 modules. §7's
  checklist is the user's to walk, and the batch is not done in the UI-contract
  sense until they do.
